# Reference — local savvy

The layer a guidebook and a hired guide cannot give. He has the tourist
information covered; this file is what a Beijinger knows. Load it whenever
recommending anything, and keep it current.

---

## 1. The calendar — check this before every recommendation

**Verified 2026-09-12.** Holiday dates drive crowds, prices, checkpoint queues,
and social obligation. Raise them proactively; never wait to be asked.

| Date | What | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun 20 Sep 2026** | ⚠️ **Adjusted WORKING day** | A Sunday that is a workday. Traffic and checkpoint run like a weekday. Classic trap. |
| **Fri 25 – Sun 27 Sep** | **中秋节 (Zhōngqiūjié) — Mid-Autumn Festival** | The family-reunion holiday. See §2 — this creates a gift obligation. Travel and restaurants jam. |
| Mon 28 – Wed 30 Sep | Normal working days | The one clear window between the two holidays |
| **Thu 1 – Wed 7 Oct** | **国庆节 (Guóqìngjié) — National Day, Golden Week** | Peak domestic travel of the year. Forbidden City effectively impossible. Checkpoint security tightens for political reasons, not just volume. Avoid all Beijing-side plans. |
| **Sat 10 Oct 2026** | ⚠️ **Adjusted WORKING day** | Same trap as 20 Sep |

## 2. 中秋节 (Zhōngqiūjié) — the gift obligation

Mid-Autumn is the **second most important family holiday after 春节 (Chūnjié)**,
Spring Festival. Its whole theme is 团圆 (tuányuán) — family reunion, the round
moon, the round cake.

**月饼 (yuèbǐng) — mooncakes — are effectively obligatory** as a household gift
around this date. This is separate from and additional to the gift already
bought for 叔叔 (shūshu):

| | |
|---|---|
| What | A boxed gift set 月饼礼盒 (yuèbǐng lǐhé), not loose cakes |
| Budget | ~¥150–400 (≈₩29,000–78,000). Cheap boxes read as cheap; very expensive ones read as showing off |
| Timing | **Give BEFORE the 25th, not on the day.** Arriving on the day with them is late. |
| **Brand — the local pick** | **北京稻香村 (Běijīng Dàoxiāngcūn)** is the 老字号 (lǎozìhào) established Beijing house for mooncakes. It is what Beijingers actually buy. Buying it says *I know what's correct here*, which at a first meeting beats being distinctive. |
| Korean angle | Bring a Korean set **as a secondary**, alongside the 稻香村 box — not instead of it. Correctness first, personal touch second. |
| Watch for | Nothing in fours. Two boxes or one good one. Hand over with **both hands**. |

If the first family meeting lands near this date, the mooncakes are not optional
courtesy — they are the price of entry.

## 3. Tourist traps — say the name, say skip

| Trap | Where | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| [王府井小吃街 (Wángfǔjǐng xiǎochījiē)](https://maps.apple.com/?q=%E7%8E%8B%E5%BA%9C%E4%BA%95%E5%B0%8F%E5%90%83%E8%A1%97) | Wangfujing | Scorpions-on-sticks theatre for tourists. **Beijingers do not eat here.** Fine to walk through, not to eat. |
| 东华门夜市 (Dōnghuámén Yèshì) | — | **Closed permanently since 2016.** Still in guidebooks and blogs. Does not exist. |
| "Tea ceremony" invitation | 天安门 (Tiān'ānmén), 王府井 (Wángfǔjǐng) | **Scam.** Friendly well-dressed young people, good English, "practising English," suggest a teahouse. Bill lands at ¥500–2,000+ per person, sometimes far worse. |
| "Art student" exhibition | 天安门 (Tiān'ānmén) area | **Scam.** Free gallery visit → high-pressure sale of worthless scrolls. |
| Unlicensed taxi 黑车 (hēichē) | Every station and tourist exit | Rigged meters, no recourse. **Use 滴滴 (Dīdī).** A real taxi has a meter, a roof light, and a visible driver ID card. |

**The pattern behind all three scams: an unprompted approach in good English
near a monument.** Locals don't do this. The correct response is to keep
walking. If you want a line: **Bù yòng, xièxie.** 不用，谢谢。 [No need, thanks.]
Said flatly, without slowing down.

## 4. Price benchmarks — researched 2026-09-12

**Confidence column is not decoration.** ✅ = official or multiple agreeing
sources. ⚠️ = single source or a range wide enough to distrust.
🔢 = derived by me from a published tariff, not observed.

### Exchange rate
**¥1 ≈ ₩202.** For mental arithmetic use **×200** — under 1% off and instant.
Checked 2026-09-12: converters clustered at ₩201.85–201.97, with outliers to
₩219; the 90-day range was ₩202–230. **Treat ±8% as the error bar** and check
the app before anything large.

¥250 for that trip is asking roughly double.

## 5. Timing — the local rhythm

| | |
|---|---|
| Lunch | **11:30–13:00.** Restaurants are slammed at 12:00 and many genuinely close 14:00–17:00. Eat at 11:30 or 13:30. |
| Dinner | **17:30–20:00.** Chinese dinner is earlier than Korean. A 20:30 arrival finds a tired kitchen. |
| Attractions | Arrive at opening or after 15:00. The 10:00–14:00 block is the crush. |
| Rush hour | ~07:00–09:30 and ~17:00–19:30. The checkpoint compounds it — see §4 of CLAUDE.md. |
| ⚠️ Weekends | Beijing sights are **worse on weekends than weekdays** by a wide margin. Push to a weekday whenever the choice exists. |

## 6. Foreigner-specific failure modes

| Failure | The savvy move |
|---|---|
| Ticketed sites need **passport-matched advance booking** | Book 7 days out. Carry the **physical passport**, not a photo. This applies far beyond the Forbidden City. |
| Beijing taxis **refuse Hebei runs** | Ask first: **Nín qù Yānjiāo ma?** 您去燕郊吗？ Or just use 滴滴 (Dīdī). |
| Foreign cards rejected nearly everywhere | 支付宝 (Zhīfùbǎo) Alipay for everything. Keep ~¥200 cash for the rare holdout. |
| Museums closed **Mondays** | Near-universal in China. Plan Mondays as a Yanjiao-side day. |
| Queueing is contact-tolerant | Standing back politely reads as not wanting your turn, not as courtesy. Close the gap. |
| Being handed a menu with no prices | Ask before ordering: **Zhège duōshao qián?** 这个多少钱？ |

## 7. Looking like you've been here a while

Small signals, disproportionate effect on how he's treated:

- **说慢一点 (shuō màn yìdiǎn)** — "speak slower" — instead of switching to English. Keeps the interaction in Mandarin and marks him as a resident, not a tourist.
- Pay by scanning **their** code, not by presenting his own. Presenting yours is the tourist move.
- **不用了，谢谢 (bú yòng le, xièxie)** to touts, flat, without eye contact or slowing.
- Carry the address in **characters** on the phone screen. Pinyin is for his mouth, characters are for their eyes.
- Know his own neighbourhood landmark — 燕达医院 (Yàndá Yīyuàn) — rather than an address. Drivers navigate by landmark.
