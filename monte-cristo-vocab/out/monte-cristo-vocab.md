# The Count of Monte Cristo - vocabulary by chapter

A reading companion for the Robin Buss translation (Penguin Classics),
whose 117 chapters follow the French original's numbering.

> These lists are curated from the novel, not extracted from Buss's text
> (see README.md, 'How this was built'). Expect the sense to match and the
> exact wording to vary.

## Core glossary

Terms that recur throughout the book. Learn these once and the whole novel
gets easier.

### History Politics

- **Bonapartist** - supporter of Napoleon; in 1815 Marseille, a dangerous thing to be called
- **royalist** - supporter of the restored Bourbon kings (Louis XVIII, Charles X)
- **the usurper** - royalist codeword for Napoleon — never named directly in polite company
- **the Corsican ogre** - hostile royalist nickname for Napoleon
- **Elba** - island off Tuscany where Napoleon was exiled in 1814; Dantes' fatal errand
- **the Hundred Days** - Mar-Jul 1815: Napoleon's return from Elba to his defeat at Waterloo
- **the Restoration** - return of the Bourbon monarchy after Napoleon; the novel's political weather
- **Saint Helena** - remote Atlantic island of Napoleon's second, final exile
- **ultra / ultra-royalist** - extreme reactionary royalist, more royalist than the king
- **Jacobin** - radical revolutionary; by 1815 a term of abuse
- **the Terror** - 1793-94 phase of the Revolution marked by mass executions
- **Carbonari** - secret Italian revolutionary society; Noirtier's old world
- **Girondin** - moderate revolutionary faction destroyed by the Jacobins
- **the Tuileries** - Paris royal palace; seat of the king's government
- **emigre** - aristocrat who fled France during the Revolution and returned after it
- **Ali Pasha / Janina** - Albanian ruler of Janina betrayed and killed in 1822; the Morcerf secret
- **Sublime Porte** - the Ottoman government

### Law Crime

- **procureur du roi** - crown prosecutor - Villefort's office; roughly a district attorney
- **deputy crown prosecutor** - the English rendering of 'substitut du procureur du roi', Villefort's rank
- **juge d'instruction** - investigating magistrate who builds a criminal case before trial
- **gendarme** - soldier-policeman of the rural/state police force
- **commissary of police** - police official who conducts arrests and searches
- **deposition** - sworn written statement of evidence
- **indictment** - formal charge of a serious crime
- **the assizes** - criminal court sitting that tries the gravest offences
- **the Chamber of Peers** - upper house of the French parliament; tries its own members
- **interdiction** - legal ruling stripping a person of control over their own affairs
- **entail / settlement** - legal arrangement fixing how property must pass to heirs
- **codicil** - an addition or amendment to a will
- **usury** - lending money at extortionate interest
- **galleys / the hulks** - convict labour sentence; by this era, prison ships and penal yards
- **banditti** - brigands, organised outlaw bands (Italian plural, used in English)
- **contraband** - smuggled goods; the smugglers' trade that saves Dantes

### Money

- **franc** - base French currency unit of the period
- **sou** - small coin, 1/20 of a franc; 'not a sou' = penniless
- **louis** - gold coin worth 20 francs
- **napoleon** - 20-franc gold coin struck under Napoleon
- **ecu** - older silver coin, commonly reckoned at 3 or 5 francs
- **livre** - pre-Revolutionary money of account, roughly succeeded by the franc
- **piastre** - Spanish/Levantine silver dollar used across the Mediterranean
- **sequin / zecchino** - Venetian gold coin; the currency of Italian bribery in the novel
- **scudo (pl. scudi)** - Roman silver coin
- **crown** - English rendering of the ecu/scudo
- **bill of exchange** - written order to pay a sum on a date; the lifeblood of Morrel's firm
- **draft / note of hand** - written promise or order to pay; presented for payment when due
- **protest (a bill)** - formally record that a bill was not paid - public proof of insolvency
- **unlimited credit** - banking arrangement letting the holder draw any sum; the Count's weapon
- **the funds / rentes** - government bonds; their price swings drive Danglars' speculation
- **speculation** - risky trading for profit on price movements
- **solvent / insolvent** - able (or unable) to pay one's debts
- **dowry** - money or property a bride brings to a marriage

### Rank Society

- **abbe** - French title for a cleric or abbot; also a learned man in holy orders
- **comte / count** - noble rank between viscount and marquis; the title Dantes buys
- **vicomte / viscount** - noble rank below a count; Albert de Morcerf's title
- **baron** - lowest rank of the titled nobility; Danglars' purchased title
- **the faubourg** - district just outside the old city walls; Faubourg Saint-Honore = wealth and rank
- **hotel** - a grand private townhouse (not an inn) - 'the Count's hotel in Paris'
- **salon** - reception room; also the social gathering held in it
- **boudoir** - a woman's private sitting room
- **livery** - uniform worn by a household's servants, in the family's colours
- **valet de chambre** - a gentleman's personal manservant
- **concierge** - doorkeeper and caretaker of a building
- **major-domo / steward** - chief servant who runs a great household - Bertuccio's post
- **parvenu** - a newly rich person lacking the manners of the class they have joined
- **nabob** - someone of conspicuous, usually foreign-made, fortune
- **cicerone** - a local guide who shows visitors the sights
- **chaperone** - older woman who accompanies a young unmarried one in public
- **betrothal** - formal engagement to marry
- **mesalliance** - a marriage beneath one's social rank

### Travel

- **post-horses** - fresh horses hired in relays at posting stations for fast travel
- **postilion** - rider who guides a carriage team from the saddle rather than a box
- **diligence** - the big public stagecoach
- **berline** - large enclosed four-wheeled travelling carriage
- **calash / caleche** - light carriage with a folding hood
- **brougham** - small closed one-horse town carriage
- **cabriolet** - light two-wheeled one-horse carriage
- **tilbury** - fast, fashionable open two-wheeled gig - the sporting young man's carriage
- **phaeton** - light open carriage driven by its owner, built for speed
- **barouche** - roomy four-wheeled carriage with a folding half-hood
- **courier** - servant sent ahead to arrange horses and lodging on a journey
- **the telegraph** - Chappe semaphore tower relaying signals across France; Danglars' undoing

### Sea

- **felucca** - small fast Mediterranean boat with triangular lateen sails
- **tartan** - single-masted Mediterranean coasting vessel
- **supercargo** - officer aboard a merchant ship in charge of the cargo and its sale
- **lateen** - triangular sail on a long slanting yard - the Mediterranean rig
- **roadstead / the roads** - sheltered water off a shore where ships may anchor
- **mizzen / topsail / bowsprit** - after mast / upper sail / spar projecting from the bow
- **to lie to / heave to** - hold a ship nearly stationary against the wind
- **quarantine** - enforced isolation of an arriving ship suspected of disease
- **lazaretto** - the quarantine station itself
- **ballast** - heavy material in the hold that keeps a ship stable
- **bill of lading** - document listing a ship's cargo and its terms of carriage
- **chandler** - dealer supplying ships with stores and gear
- **the Pharaon** - Morrel's merchantman - Dantes' ship, and the firm's fortune

### Italy

- **mazzolata** - Roman execution by a blow of the mace, followed by the throat-cutting
- **mandaia** - the Roman guillotine
- **moccoletto** - little candle carried on the last night of Carnival; the game is to blow out others'
- **osteria** - a simple Italian inn or tavern
- **Trastevere** - Roman district across the Tiber, of rough reputation
- **the Corso** - Rome's main street, centre of the Carnival
- **Piazza del Popolo** - Roman square where public executions were staged
- **catacombs** - underground burial galleries; Vampa's headquarters
- **contadino** - an Italian peasant or countryman
- **Ave Maria** - the evening bell; in Rome, the hour that ends the day's business
- **brigand** - armed robber of the roads and hills
- **Colosseum** - the Roman amphitheatre; a night meeting place in the novel

### Recurring Hard Words

- **cupidity** - greed, especially for money
- **obsequious** - fawning, servile in a way that grates
- **sardonic** - mocking, bitterly scornful
- **impassive** - showing no emotion whatever - the Count's default
- **livid** - of a face: ashen, leaden, drained of colour (not primarily 'angry')
- **ineffable** - too great to be put into words
- **implacable** - impossible to appease or soften - the novel's key word for revenge
- **inexorable** - unstoppable, that cannot be persuaded to turn aside
- **providence** - God's ordering of events; the Count's claim to be its agent
- **expiation** - atonement, paying for a sin by suffering
- **perfidy** - treachery, breach of faith
- **calumny** - slander, a damaging lie told about someone
- **denunciation** - an accusation made to the authorities - the letter that destroys Dantes
- **anonymous** - of unknown authorship; the form the denunciation takes
- **dissimulation** - concealing one's true feelings or intentions
- **affability** - easy, pleasant friendliness of manner
- **hauteur** - haughtiness, cold superiority of manner
- **sang-froid** - cool self-possession under pressure
- **nonchalance** - studied casualness, the appearance of not caring
- **ennui** - listless boredom, world-weariness
- **melancholy** - settled, thoughtful sadness
- **prodigious** - enormous, astonishing in scale
- **munificence** - extraordinary generosity in giving
- **largesse** - money or gifts given freely, from a position of power
- **solicitude** - anxious care and concern for someone
- **importunate** - persistently demanding, pestering
- **vicissitude** - a turn of fortune, a sudden change of circumstances
- **presentiment** - a foreboding, a feeling that something is about to happen
- **countenance** - the face, or its expression; also 'to countenance' = to permit
- **physiognomy** - a person's facial features, read as a guide to character
- **interlocutor** - the person one is speaking with
- **colloquy** - a conversation, especially a formal one
- **apostrophe** - breaking off to address someone or something directly
- **peroration** - the grand closing section of a speech
- **lacuna** - a gap, a missing piece - as in the burnt half of a document
- **pecuniary** - having to do with money
- **avarice** - extreme miserly greed
- **probity** - complete honesty and uprightness - Morrel's defining quality
- **magnanimity** - greatness of spirit, generosity toward a rival or enemy
- **opprobrium** - public disgrace and harsh criticism
- **ignominy** - deep public shame and humiliation
- **obloquy** - verbal abuse and the bad name it leaves behind
- **recrimination** - answering an accusation with a counter-accusation
- **importuning** - badgering someone with requests
- **alacrity** - brisk and cheerful readiness
- **assiduity** - close, persistent attention to a task
- **punctilious** - exact about small points of correct behaviour
- **supercilious** - coolly contemptuous, looking down on others
- **effrontery** - shameless boldness
- **temerity** - reckless nerve in doing something risky
- **torpor** - sluggish inactivity, the dullness of a body barely alive
- **lassitude** - weariness, lack of energy
- **delirium** - wandering, feverish disorder of the mind
- **catalepsy** - seizure leaving the body rigid and unresponsive - Faria's affliction
- **apoplexy** - a stroke; sudden loss of speech and movement - Noirtier's condition
- **paralytic** - unable to move; Noirtier, who speaks only with his eyes
- **somnambulism** - sleepwalking; also the trance state of mesmerism
- **narcotic / soporific** - a drug that dulls the senses / that brings sleep
- **hashish** - cannabis preparation; the Count's drug of visions
- **brucine** - poison related to strychnine, given in rising doses; the Villefort thread
- **antidote** - substance that counteracts a poison
- **toxicology** - the study of poisons - a subject the Count has mastered

## By chapter

### 1. Marseille - The Arrival

*The Pharaon enters Marseille harbour, February 1815*

- **three-master** (nautical) - ship with three masts; the Pharaon's rig
- **roadstead** (nautical) - sheltered anchorage off the shore
- **pilot** (nautical) - local expert who steers a ship through a harbour entrance
- **supercargo** (nautical) - officer in charge of a merchant ship's cargo and its sale - Danglars' job
- **owner / shipowner** (nautical) - Morrel, who owns the vessel and employs its crew
- **brain fever** (medicine/poison) - period term for a fatal inflammation of the brain - Captain Leclere's death
- **Chateau d'If** (history) - island fortress-prison in the bay of Marseille
- **Notre-Dame de la Garde** (French) - hilltop church overlooking the harbour; the lookout point
- **Porto Ferrajo** (history) - chief port of Elba - the stop that will damn Dantes
- **to put in / lie to** (nautical) - to call at a port / to hold a ship still against the wind
- **quarantine** (nautical) - isolation imposed on an arriving ship suspected of carrying disease
- **mate** (nautical) - officer ranking below the captain; Dantes' rank before promotion

### 2. Father and Son

*Dantes finds his father half-starved in the Allees de Meilhan*

- **nasturtium / clematis** (general) - climbing plants on the old man's window - a small, tended dignity
- **garret** (general) - cramped room under a roof; poverty's address
- **penury** (general) - extreme poverty, want of the necessities of life
- **abstinence** (general) - going without - here, the father starving to pay a debt
- **creditor** (money/finance) - one to whom money is owed; Caderousse's claim
- **filial** (general) - of a son or daughter toward a parent
- **solicitude** (general) - anxious, tender concern
- **envy** (general) - the novel's engine - watch Caderousse's tone curdle
- **advance / on account** (money/finance) - money paid before it is earned
- **hypocrisy** (general) - professing feelings one does not have - Caderousse's congratulations

### 3. The Catalans

*The Catalan fishing village; Mercedes, Fernand, and the arbour at La Reserve*

- **the Catalans** (history) - colony of Spanish fisherfolk settled outside Marseille; Mercedes' people
- **arbour** (general) - shaded garden seat under trained vines - where the plot is hatched
- **trellis** (general) - lattice frame supporting climbing vines
- **cousin** (general) - Fernand's claim on Mercedes - kinship used as a lever
- **conscription** (history) - compulsory military service; Fernand's looming call-up
- **rival** (general) - the word Danglars plants in Fernand's ear
- **sullen** (general) - silently resentful, gloomily bad-tempered
- **vehemence** (general) - forceful intensity of feeling or speech
- **importunity** (general) - persistent, unwelcome pressing of a suit
- **cabaret** (French) - small tavern (not a nightclub in this era)
- **insinuate** (general) - to suggest something damaging without stating it outright - Danglars' whole method

### 4. Conspiracy

*Danglars writes the denunciation with his left hand*

- **denunciation** (law/courts) - accusation laid before the authorities - the letter that destroys Dantes
- **anonymous** (general) - unsigned, of concealed authorship
- **informer** (law/courts) - one who secretly reports another to the authorities
- **Bonapartist agent** (history) - the charge invented against Dantes
- **disguised hand** (general) - altered handwriting meant to defeat identification
- **in his cups** (archaic) - drunk - Caderousse's alibi for consenting
- **connivance** (general) - silent cooperation in a wrong by not preventing it
- **malice** (general) - the wish to do harm for its own sake
- **pretext** (general) - a false reason offered to conceal the real one
- **usurper** (history) - royalist codeword for Napoleon, used in the letter

### 5. The Marriage Feast

*The betrothal breakfast at La Reserve, broken by the gendarmes*

- **betrothal feast** (general) - celebration of a formal engagement
- **nuptials** (literary/allusion) - the wedding ceremony
- **banns** (general) - public announcement of an intended marriage
- **commissary of police** (law/courts) - the official who makes the arrest
- **gendarme** (French) - soldier-policeman
- **warrant** (law/courts) - written authority to arrest
- **in the king's name** (law/courts) - the formula that opens a lawful arrest
- **consternation** (general) - dismay that stops people where they stand
- **presentiment** (general) - a foreboding - Mercedes feels it before the knock
- **exultation** (general) - triumphant joy - Danglars', carefully hidden

### 6. The Deputy Crown Prosecutor

*Villefort's own betrothal to Renee de Saint-Meran*

- **deputy crown prosecutor** (law/courts) - Villefort's office - assistant state prosecutor
- **marquis / marquise** (French) - high noble rank; Renee's arch-royalist parents
- **the Saint-Merans** (history) - ultra-royalist family whose favour Villefort is marrying into
- **regicide** (law/courts) - a killer of a king - the taint on Villefort's father Noirtier
- **abjure** (general) - to renounce formally - Villefort has abjured his father's politics
- **ambition** (general) - Villefort's governing passion; read every kindness through it
- **clemency** (law/courts) - mercy shown by one with power to punish
- **zeal** (general) - eager, demonstrative devotion to a cause - the currency of the Restoration
- **Noirtier** (history) - Villefort's Bonapartist father; the name he cannot afford to own
- **sinecure** (general) - a post that pays without demanding work

### 7. The Interrogation

*Villefort questions Dantes and burns the letter*

- **interrogation** (law/courts) - formal questioning of a suspect
- **deposition** (law/courts) - the sworn record of what a witness says
- **candour** (general) - frank openness - Dantes' fatal honesty
- **exculpate** (law/courts) - to clear of blame
- **incriminate** (law/courts) - to make someone appear guilty
- **the marechal / Marshal Bertrand** (history) - Napoleon's officer on Elba who gave Dantes the letter
- **Rue Coq-Heron** (French) - the Paris address on the letter - Noirtier's
- **self-preservation** (general) - the instinct that turns Villefort from judge into jailer
- **pallor** (general) - unnatural paleness - Villefort's, when he reads the name
- **expedient** (general) - a convenient device, chosen for advantage rather than principle

### 8. The Chateau d'If

*Dantes is rowed out to the island fortress*

- **dungeon / donjon** (archaic) - the fortified keep; also the cell beneath it
- **turnkey** (archaic) - a jailer, one who turns the locks
- **governor (of a prison)** (law/courts) - the officer commanding the prison
- **state prisoner** (law/courts) - one held for political reasons, often without trial
- **incommunicado** (law/courts) - held without contact with anyone outside
- **oubliette** (French) - dungeon whose prisoners are 'forgotten' - the fourth-class cell
- **grating** (general) - iron bars across a window or opening
- **mutinous** (general) - rebellious against authority - how Dantes' protest is read
- **resignation** (general) - acceptance of what cannot be changed
- **despair** (general) - the chapter's destination; note how slowly Dumas gets there

### 9. The Evening of the Betrothal

*Villefort leaves for Paris; Morrel tries to intervene*

- **post-chaise** (general) - fast hired carriage for long journeys
- **audience** (general) - a formal interview granted by a superior or a monarch
- **intercede** (general) - to plead on another's behalf - Morrel for Dantes
- **importune** (general) - to press a request beyond welcome
- **evasive** (general) - avoiding a direct answer - Villefort's whole manner
- **credentials** (general) - documents proving one's authority or identity
- **the Faubourg Saint-Germain** (French) - Paris quarter of the old aristocracy
- **protege** (French) - one whose career is advanced by a patron
- **equivocate** (general) - to use language deliberately open to two meanings

### 10. The Little Room in the Tuileries

*Louis XVIII, the Duc de Blacas, and the police minister*

- **the Tuileries** (history) - royal palace in Paris; the king's private cabinet
- **Louis XVIII** (history) - the restored Bourbon king, gouty and bookish
- **Duc de Blacas** (history) - the king's alarmed favourite
- **minister of police** (law/courts) - head of the state's political surveillance
- **Horace** (literary/allusion) - Roman poet the king quotes to show composure
- **cabinet** (general) - a monarch's small private room; also his inner council
- **dispatch** (general) - an official message sent with urgency
- **complacency** (general) - smug satisfaction that blinds one to danger
- **insurrection** (history) - an armed rising against authority
- **pedantry** (general) - showy fussiness about learning - the king's defence against panic

### 11. The Corsican Ogre

*News arrives that Napoleon has landed in France*

- **the Corsican ogre** (history) - royalist epithet for Napoleon
- **Gulf of Juan** (history) - where Napoleon landed on 1 March 1815
- **Grenoble / Lyons** (history) - cities that opened their gates to him on the march north
- **defection** (history) - abandoning one side for another - the army's, regiment by regiment
- **proclamation** (general) - public official announcement
- **rout** (general) - a disorderly collapse and flight
- **consternation** (general) - dismay at sudden bad news
- **vacillate** (general) - to waver between courses of action
- **Ghent** (history) - Belgian city the king flees to

### 12. Father and Son (Noirtier and Villefort)

*Noirtier arrives in disguise; the killing of General Quesnel*

- **Bonapartist club** (history) - secret political society plotting the return
- **Rue Saint-Jacques** (French) - address of the club where Quesnel vanishes
- **General Quesnel** (history) - royalist officer killed leaving the club - Noirtier's duel
- **duel** (general) - formal armed combat over honour; here, one without seconds
- **the Seine** (French) - the river the body is found in
- **cockade** (history) - ribbon rosette worn to show political allegiance - white for the king, tricolour for Napoleon
- **queue** (archaic) - the old-fashioned pigtail Noirtier cuts off to change his appearance
- **incognito** (Italian) - with identity concealed
- **collusion** (law/courts) - secret cooperation for a dishonest purpose - father and son, at last
- **compromising** (general) - of evidence: damaging to one's safety or reputation

### 13. The Hundred Days

*Napoleon returns; Morrel petitions; Villefort survives the turn*

- **the Hundred Days** (history) - Napoleon's brief second reign, March to July 1815
- **Waterloo** (history) - the battle that ends it
- **the Second Restoration** (history) - the Bourbons' return after Waterloo - and Dantes' doom sealed
- **petition** (law/courts) - formal written plea to authority - Morrel's, twice made and twice lost
- **amnesty** (law/courts) - general pardon for political offences
- **the White Terror** (history) - royalist reprisals against Bonapartists after Waterloo
- **purge** (history) - removal of the politically suspect from office
- **turncoat** (general) - one who switches allegiance for advantage
- **expediency** (general) - doing what is advantageous rather than what is right
- **oblivion** (general) - being utterly forgotten - the fate arranged for Dantes

### 14. The Raving Prisoner and the Mad Prisoner

*The inspector's tour of the Chateau d'If*

- **inspector of prisons** (law/courts) - visiting official who hears prisoners' complaints
- **register** (law/courts) - the prison's book of entries - where Dantes' name is buried
- **Number 34** (law/courts) - how the prison names Dantes; he has lost even his name
- **raving** (general) - wild and incoherent from madness or fever
- **the mad abbe** (general) - how the staff dismiss Faria and his talk of treasure
- **millions** (money/finance) - Faria's offered ransom - proof, to the jailers, that he is insane
- **petition** (law/courts) - prisoner's written plea for review of his case
- **perfunctory** (general) - done as a formality, without real care
- **indefinite detention** (law/courts) - imprisonment without a fixed term or trial
- **callous** (general) - hardened, unfeeling toward suffering

### 15. Number 34 and Number 27

*Dantes hears digging and meets the Abbe Faria*

- **subterranean** (general) - underground
- **gallery** (general) - a tunnel or long passage
- **flagstone** (general) - flat paving slab - the floor Dantes levers up
- **mortar** (general) - the cement binding stonework, scraped away in secret
- **pickaxe / chisel** (general) - improvised tools made from a saucepan handle and bedstead
- **buttress** (general) - stone support built against a wall
- **reprieve** (law/courts) - a suspension of punishment; here, the reprieve of company
- **hallucination** (medicine/poison) - what Dantes first assumes the sound must be
- **emaciated** (medicine/poison) - wasted thin by starvation or illness
- **bedstead** (general) - the frame of a bed - a source of iron

### 16. A Learned Italian

*Faria's history, his learning, his manufactured tools*

- **the abbe** (French) - Faria - priest, scholar, engineer, and the novel's true father figure
- **secretary (to a cardinal)** (general) - a scholar-servant managing a great man's papers
- **Cardinal Spada** (Italian) - the Roman family whose lost treasure Faria has traced
- **polyglot** (general) - speaking many languages - Faria's five
- **treatise** (literary/allusion) - a formal written work on a subject
- **manuscript** (literary/allusion) - a handwritten book - Faria's, on strips of shirt linen
- **ingenuity** (general) - cleverness at contriving things from nothing
- **soot and wine** (general) - Faria's improvised ink
- **cartilage** (general) - fish gristle used to make his pens
- **the unification of Italy** (history) - Faria's political dream, decades ahead of its time

### 17. The Abbe's Cell

*Faria reconstructs the conspiracy; Dantes' education begins*

- **deduction** (general) - reasoning from evidence to a conclusion - Faria's demonstration
- **cui bono** (literary/allusion) - 'who benefits?' - the Latin question that unmasks Danglars and Fernand
- **motive** (law/courts) - the reason behind an act
- **revelation** (general) - sudden disclosure of a hidden truth
- **vengeance** (general) - the idea that enters Dantes here and never leaves
- **curriculum** (general) - the course of study Faria sets him
- **mathematics, physics, history** (general) - the syllabus that makes the Count possible
- **aptitude** (general) - natural ability to learn something
- **metamorphosis** (literary/allusion) - complete transformation - the sailor becoming the scholar
- **rancour** (general) - long-nursed bitterness

### 18. The Treasure

*The Spada parchment and its burnt second half*

- **parchment** (general) - prepared animal skin used for durable documents
- **Alexander VI / Borgia** (history) - the pope who poisoned cardinals for their estates
- **Caesar Borgia** (history) - the pope's son; the threat that made Spada hide the gold
- **breviary** (literary/allusion) - priest's book of daily prayers - where the paper was concealed
- **invisible ink / sympathetic ink** (general) - writing revealed only by heat
- **legacy / bequest** (law/courts) - property left by will
- **lacuna** (literary/allusion) - the gap where the parchment burned away
- **conjecture** (general) - reasoned guess filling in missing evidence
- **grotto** (Italian) - a cave - the treasure's hiding place on Monte Cristo
- **ingot** (money/finance) - a cast bar of gold or silver

### 19. The Third Attack

*Faria's final seizure and death*

- **catalepsy** (medicine/poison) - seizure leaving the body rigid and insensible
- **apoplexy** (medicine/poison) - a stroke
- **paroxysm** (medicine/poison) - a sudden violent attack of a disease
